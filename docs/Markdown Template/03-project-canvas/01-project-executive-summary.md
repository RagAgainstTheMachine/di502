# Project Executive Summary: Türkiye AI Policy Assistant (DRAFT v0.7)

> Draft prepared on 6 October 2026 for team review. Items in **[brackets]** are open: values to be measured, people to be named, or targets the team must confirm. All target levels below are **proposals** and must be confirmed or revised before the charter is baselined at the end of Sprint 1.

## Background

In August 2026, Presidential Circular 2026/9 put the Türkiye AI Action Plan (2026–2030) into force for public institutions [1, 2]. Under Action 7 of the plan, every public institution must appoint a senior executive responsible for AI transformation and, within 12 weeks of these appointments, prepare a three-year AI roadmap [2]. The rules this work depends on are published separately by eight official sources: an action plan (44 pages), the personal data protection law (21 pages) and its generative-AI guide (64 pages), a parliamentary AI commission report (923 pages), the 2026 Presidential Annual Programme with its AI-related measures, sectoral and institutional AI policies, and four AI bills at commission stage [2–6, 8]. These documents range from binding law to non-binding plans, recommendations and bills; Türkiye has no AI-specific law yet, and responsibility for AI governance moved between institutions as recently as December 2025 [5]. Today, staff who are not AI-policy specialists must find these documents, read the relevant parts, and work out for themselves which rules bind their institution and which are only plans or bills.

**Budget:** $100 of Google Cloud credits (fixed, per project). **Duration:** 4 sprints. **Timeline:** 14 October 2026 to 30 December 2026.

**Conflicts between stakeholders:**

- **Users (1) vs regulators (5, 6):** users want yes/no answers to case-specific questions. The authority for binding decisions stays with regulators and the institution's legal unit. *Resolution:* the assistant explains the applicable rules and refers case-specific assessments to the competent authority.
- **Source documents (4, 7) disagree:** e.g. the commission report advises against short-term changes to criminal law, while bill 2/3358 proposes them. *Resolution:* the assistant presents each position in the document collection with its source and legal status, and does not decide between them.
- **Customer (3) vs users (1, 2):** the commissioning ministry may prefer its own policy documents to be emphasised, while users need a neutral picture that also covers parliamentary recommendations that differ from current practice. *Resolution:* the assistant presents every source with its issuer and legal status and favours none. The Ministry holds two roles (rows 3 and 5), recorded in separate rows.
- **Primary (1) vs secondary users (2):** researchers may also want history and comparisons between strategies. *Resolution:* both groups can ask about the current documents; comparisons with earlier strategies are out of scope (see Major Activities).

## 2. Project Vision and Objectives

### 2.1 Project vision

Staff in Turkish public institutions, and the researchers who study AI policy, can find what Türkiye's AI policy says in one place and in their own words, and can tell binding rules apart from plans, recommendations and bills.

> **Status:** Background, Stakeholders and Vision agreed by Batuhan on 6 October 2026. Objectives revised on 6 October 2026 (v0.5): 10 pilot participants, test set of 30 in-scope + 10 out-of-scope questions, neutral participant wording, all objectives owned by the Product Owner (Archetype 1). v0.6: scope exclusion for unpublished implementation guidance added. v0.7 (6 Oct 2026, decided by Batuhan): primary user redefined as staff who must understand and explain the policy; the assistant works in Turkish; MEB plan, the four AI bills and the 2021–2025 Strategy kept as reserve documents.

## 3. Project Scope

### 3.1 Scope Definition

A question-answering assistant in Turkish for Türkiye's current AI policy documents, used through a web page by staff in the strategy, legal and training units of public institutions who must understand and explain Türkiye's AI policy.


| # | Feature                             | What the user can do with it                                                                                                       | Objective(s) it serves |
| - | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| 1 | Plain-language questions            | Ask about Türkiye's AI policy in their own words and get an answer drawn from the official documents                              | 1, 2                   |
| 2 | Source reference                    | See the document and section or article each answer comes from, and open the original                                              | 2                      |
| 3 | Legal status label                  | See, for each cited source, whether it is a law, binding on the public sector, guidance, a plan, a report or a bill, with its date | 4                      |
| 4 | Follow-up questions                 | Ask a follow-up question in the same conversation without repeating the context                                                    | 1, 2, 3                |
| 5 | Referral for unanswerable questions | Get a pointer to the competent authority when the question is out of scope or needs a case-specific legal assessment               | 5                      |
| 6 | Collection date                     | See the date up to which the document collection is current                                                                        | 4                      |

Exclusions (case-specific legal assessments, predictions about the final AI law, each institution's role in the not-yet-published responsibility matrix, step-by-step implementation guidance that the Action Plan promises but the responsible institutions have not yet published (e.g. roadmap templates, checklists, impact-assessment templates), whether the plan applies to a specific body, institutions' internal rules, foreign regulation, changes since the 2021–2025 strategy) are listed as **Out of Scope** under Major Activities. For unpublished guidance, the assistant states that it has not been published yet and cites where the Action Plan promises it.
